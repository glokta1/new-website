---
title: "On Aligarh"
pubDate: 2025-11-26
---

I've had a difficult relationship with Aligarh from the beginning. It was never in my plans, and only at the insistence of my mother did I drag myself half asleep to take the entrance exam. First semester was online and second semester was spent dealing with the oppressive summer heat of May, looking for PGs and lowering my food expectations. I scoffed at older cousins and relatives who told me I'd miss this place when I graduate. How could I ever miss *this* place? I counted down the days till I could go back home and retreat into the comfort of my room. I whinged about the tasteless food, the mind-numbing subjects, the suffocating classrooms, the dull professors, the strange mess rules and the ten thousand times I had to fill in the same details on every form.


And yet, every night for the past week, I've gone to sleep terrified of leaving this place and the friends who've made it home. For the first time, I wish I could stay here a little longer. The prospect of home isn't as enticing this time around, replaced by a cold realisation that there is no next time – not in this place and not with these friends. Each moment in these past few weeks has carried the quiet sting of good bye. The last football matches, the final days of college, the ritual Sunday dinners.


I'll miss our 2am campus rides and its cold breeze. I'll miss the easy access to each other's rooms, joking around and poking fun for hours. I'll miss the walk to Zakaria for Tariq bhai's chai.


My clothes lie scattered all over the bed, the empty suitcase staring at me. But I do not want to pack my things. I wish to stay here in this room just a little longer.
